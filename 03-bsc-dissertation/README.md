## Research objective

*Research on a user experience quality model based on statistical analysis* investigates how the quality of user experience on an online-course platform can be assessed through structured quantitative indicators and subjective questionnaire responses.

The dissertation develops an evaluation approach combining an indicator hierarchy and empirical statistical analysis.

## Study design and methodology

- Designed a user questionnaire with **five-point ratings** to gather respondents' evaluations of an online learning experience.
- Collected **155 questionnaire responses**; **114 valid responses** were retained for the reported statistical analysis.
- Considered dimensions related to users, the environment and the service; analysed quality indicators relating to **system, information and interaction quality**.
- Applied questionnaire **reliability analysis**, correlations, **exploratory factor analysis** and related tests in **SPSS 22.0**.
- Used **MATLAB** for calculations, fitting or comparison of model-based and subjective scores.
- Used the **Mean Opinion Score (MOS)** concept in discussing subjective measurement, and considered **Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)** principles for structuring evaluation criteria.

See the [dissertation PDF](Research%20on%20a%20user%20experience%20quality%20model%20based%20on%20statistical%20analysis.pdf) for the exact questionnaire, statistical tables, calculation procedures and reported model.

## Findings reported in the dissertation

- The analysis organised **18 service-quality indicators into seven factor categories**, grouped within system, information and interaction quality.
- The questionnaire's reported **Cronbach's alpha was 0.840**, supporting internal consistency in the evaluated responses.
- The study combined factor-derived measures to produce composite service-quality assessments.
- Patterns in the calculated model scores were reported as broadly consistent with corresponding subjective user ratings in this case study.

These observations concern this specific dataset and evaluation setting; they should not be interpreted as universal validation of a general-purpose user-experience model.

## Limitations and interpretation

- The valid sample was **small (114)** and predominantly consisted of university students.
- Questionnaire ratings reflect subjective experiences and may be influenced by respondents' expectations and context.
- The reliability and evaluation findings should be tested in broader and more diverse settings before generalisation.

This research developed my interest in **measurement definitions, statistical reliability and the challenges of representing human experience through numerical indicators**.
