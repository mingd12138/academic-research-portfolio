02 | MSc Dissertation — Linguistic Style, Sentiment and NLP in WIRED

Full dissertation: Read the original PDF

Institution: Durham University

Degree: Master of Data Science

Submission: September 2024

Research type: Completed MSc dissertation

Research focus

Linguistic Style and Sentiment Analysis of Libertarianism in Cyberspace at WIRED Magazine Based on Natural Language Processing examines how themes, linguistic characteristics and sentiment associated with libertarianism in cyberspace appear in WIRED magazine articles, including variation across topics and time.

The project combines exploratory linguistic analysis, topic modelling, lexicon-based sentiment analysis and experiments with emotion-classification methods.

Data and analysis

  • Gathered and consolidated archived WIRED article records sourced through ProQuest, with material covering approximately 2008–2024.
  • Applied keyword screening to identify 2,466 potentially relevant records from a larger working corpus of approximately 4,000 records.
  • Prepared text for NLP analysis, including cleaning and preprocessing.
  • Examined linguistic characteristics and thematic structure using exploratory analysis and Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA).
  • Applied VADER and TextBlob to investigate sentiment polarity.
  • Explored emotion classification with methods including Naive Bayes, SVM, CNN, LSTM and a BERT-based approach, using labelled emotion data for model development and then analysing predictions on WIRED texts.

The full methodology, tables, figures and any code excerpts are documented in the dissertation PDF.

Findings reported in the dissertation

  • The lexicon-based results indicated that positive sentiment appeared more frequently than negative sentiment in the selected texts, while varying across periods and themes.
  • Topic modelling identified themes spanning technology, data applications, and social and cultural discussions.
  • In the reported emotion-classification analysis, the CNN output covered more of the seven reported emotion categories than the LSTM and Transformer implementations used in this project; it was consequently used for further category-level examination.

Methodological limitations

  • Corpus selection: keyword choices influence what enters the analysis; the selected subset does not represent every possible discussion of libertarianism.
  • Data provenance: archived records may have incomplete metadata or other limitations.
  • Domain shift: results learned or evaluated on one labelled emotion dataset may not transfer reliably to WIRED articles.
  • Interpretation: emotion-category definitions, sentiment lexicons and language context affect how automated outputs should be read.
  • Ground truth: predicted emotion distributions alone cannot show which classifier is most accurate on target articles without suitable independent annotations.

These limitations are important to my subsequent interest in evaluation validity, robustness and responsible interpretation of NLP outputs.

My contribution

I performed the corpus preparation and screening, NLP analyses, modelling experiments, comparison and interpretation of outputs, and dissertation writing.

03 | BSc Dissertation — Statistical Evaluation of User Experience Quality

Full dissertation: Read the original PDF (Chinese version)

Institution: Shenzhen University

Programme: Mathematics and Applied Mathematics (Financial Mathematics)

Date: 2021

Research type: Completed undergraduate dissertation

Research objective

Research on a user experience quality model based on statistical analysis investigates how the quality of user experience on an online-course platform can be assessed through structured quantitative indicators and subjective questionnaire responses.

The dissertation develops an evaluation approach combining an indicator hierarchy and empirical statistical analysis.

Study design and methodology

  • Designed a user questionnaire with five-point ratings to gather respondents' evaluations of an online learning experience.
  • Collected 155 questionnaire responses; 114 valid responses were retained for the reported statistical analysis.
  • Considered dimensions related to users, the environment and the service; analysed quality indicators relating to system, information and interaction quality.
  • Applied questionnaire reliability analysis, correlations, exploratory factor analysis and related tests in SPSS 22.0.
  • Used MATLAB for calculations, fitting or comparison of model-based and subjective scores.
  • Used the Mean Opinion Score (MOS) concept in discussing subjective measurement, and considered Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) principles for structuring evaluation criteria.

See the dissertation PDF for the exact questionnaire, statistical tables, calculation procedures and reported model.

Findings reported in the dissertation

  • The analysis organised 18 service-quality indicators into seven factor categories, grouped within system, information and interaction quality.
  • The questionnaire's reported Cronbach's alpha was 0.840, supporting internal consistency in the evaluated responses.
  • The study combined factor-derived measures to produce composite service-quality assessments.
  • Patterns in the calculated model scores were reported as broadly consistent with corresponding subjective user ratings in this case study.

Limitations and interpretation

  • The valid sample was small (114) and predominantly consisted of university students.
  • Questionnaire ratings reflect subjective experiences and may be influenced by respondents' expectations and context.
  • The reliability and evaluation findings should be tested in broader and more diverse settings before generalisation.

This research developed my interest in measurement definitions, statistical reliability and the challenges of representing human experience through numerical indicators.

My contribution

I designed the questionnaire, collected responses, performed statistical reliability and factor analyses, examined the indicator framework, interpreted the results and prepared the dissertation.
